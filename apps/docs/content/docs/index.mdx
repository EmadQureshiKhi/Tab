Tab is post-paid billing and a credit facility for autonomous agents, on Monad.

A Service meters usage into an Open Tab.
The Agent settles later, in USDC or AUSD, on the same chain, with keys nobody else holds.
The payment and the ledger entry are one state change.

The last sentence is the whole product.

<Steps>
  <Step n="01" title="The work is delivered first">
    An Agent calls a metered tool. Nothing is prepaid, no balance is checked against a deposit, and no payment stands between the request and the result.
  </Step>
  <Step n="02" title="The charge lands on an Open Tab">
    The Service records the delivery in `TabBook`. The tab is the record of what is owed, and it is readable by anyone.
  </Step>
  <Step n="03" title="The Agent settles with its own keys">
    The Agent calls `TabSettlement.settle` with the Service, the Asset and an amount. Nothing in Tab can move an Agent's funds, because nothing in Tab ever holds them.
  </Step>
  <Step n="04" title="The transfer and the ledger entry are one transaction">
    `TabSettlement` calls `TabBook.applySettlement` and moves the Asset from the Agent to the Service's Collection address in the same transaction. If the transfer fails the tab does not fall; if the book refuses the Settlement, the transfer does not happen.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Nobody asserts that money arrived

Every system that bills after the fact has to answer one question: who says the payment happened.
The usual answers are a payment facilitator, an oracle, a bridge, a relayer, or a signed webhook.
All five are the same answer wearing different clothes, which is that some party is trusted to make the claim, and that party can lie or be compromised.

Tab does not answer the question, because it does not ask it.

`TabSettlement` is the one way a tab is paid.
It calls `TabBook.applySettlement` and moves the Asset with `transferFrom`, straight from the Agent to the Collection address the Service registered, all inside one transaction.
No component of Tab is trusted to say a Settlement occurred, because the chain applied the payment itself.
There is deliberately no such component to compromise.

A Settlement is final when its block is, and Monad finalises a block in under a second.
Headroom is restored in the block the Settlement lands.
Each Settlement is identified by the `settlementId` that `TabBook` assigns, and that identifier is what the explorer, the read API and the `Settled` event all carry.

The [threat model](/threat-model) states the trusted set in full, along with the bound on what each member can do.
The short version is that the trusted parties can admit a counterparty, meter inside an authorisation, serve a stale figure, or waste their own Bond.
None of them can claim that money arrived.

## The removal test

The test that decides whether a component is load-bearing or decorative is to remove it and see what breaks.

Remove the registry read API and every figure it served is still on chain, so the Dashboard goes dark and nothing about anyone's credit changes.
Remove the metering gateway and a Service can call `TabBook.recordDelivery` from its own operator key.
Remove `TabSettlement` and no tab can ever fall, because `applySettlement` answers to that one wired address and nothing else.

That is the shape on purpose.
Everything off chain is a convenience over a ledger that does not need it.

## Why post-paid

Prepayment is the obvious way to bill an agent and it is the wrong way.

An agent that must fund an account before its first call cannot start work it was dispatched to do.
An agent that must top up mid-task stalls at the moment it is least able to ask for help.
Prepayment also puts the Service in custody of the Agent's money, which is a liability the Service did not want and a risk the Agent did not agree to.

Post-paid billing inverts all three.
The Agent calls first and settles afterwards, holding its own funds the entire time.
The Service carries the balance for a Settlement Window and is protected by a Credit Limit rather than by a deposit.

Credit is the hard part, and it is where the Bond comes in.
An Agent's Credit Limit is computed from its own Settlement history and from Bond that Services have escrowed, so extending credit costs the party extending it.
A ring of colluding accounts cannot manufacture credit without locking strictly more value than the credit it unlocks.

## What the chain records, and what Tab reads

The Credit Limit is a pure function of onchain history.
Its inputs are the Agent's ordered Settlement history in one Asset, rebuilt from `HistoryExtended` logs and folded to the `historyCommitment` that `TabBook` stores, plus one Bond entry per counterparty Service, read from `Bond` at the moment of the call.

Three consequences shape the design rather than being papered over.

Credit is backed by Service Bonds and never by Agent balances, so no balance of the Agent's is ever read and no balance needs to be.
An Agent with no counterparties has no Credit Limit; its first Settlement banks prepaid credit and creates the history a limit grows from.
A tab that outlives its Settlement Window can be marked delinquent by anyone, and a mark zeroes the Agent's credit in that Asset rather than seizing anything from anyone.

The [boundaries page](/limitations) carries the rest, each with the bound that says how far it reaches.

## Where it runs

Tab is live on Monad Mainnet and on Monad Testnet, as two deployments of the same contracts.
Each deployment lives on one chain, gas is paid in MON, and every amount anywhere in the system is an integer count of base units.

| --- | --- |

The demo Service `tab.demo` is registered on both: on Testnet with a Bond of 50 mUSDC and 20 USDC and prices in both Testnet Assets, holding ERC-8004 identity `1913` beside the demo Agent's `1914`; on Mainnet with a 1 USDC Bond and prices in both USDC and AUSD, holding `10254` beside the Agent's `10255`.

The curation role is the one difference between the two that is not an address.
On Testnet the deploying account holds it.
On Mainnet a 2-of-3 `CurationMultisig` does, deployed before the registry because the authority is a constructor argument with no setter.
The [boundaries page](/limitations#one-authority-decides-the-curated-tier) says who holds its three keys today.
